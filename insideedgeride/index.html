Why personalised zones matter

Train for a purpose, not simply for more fatigue.

Training zones divide effort into useful intensity ranges based on your own physiology. Instead of following a generic wattage or heart-rate target, Inside Edge Ride uses your FTP, LTHR and body weight to make the guidance personal to you.

That makes structured training more precise. Endurance sessions can stay aerobic, threshold work can target sustainable power, and harder efforts can develop VO2 max, anaerobic capacity or sprint performance without every ride becoming the same medium-hard effort.

As fitness changes, updating FTP and LTHR updates the zones too. The numbers remain challenging and relevant, while giving you a consistent reference for monitoring progress and avoiding accidental undertraining or overtraining.

Two views of the same rider

Power shows the work. Heart rate shows the response.

FTP and LTHR describe different parts of cycling performance. Inside Edge Ride brings them together so you can understand both what you are producing and how your body is responding.

FTP

Functional Threshold Power

FTP is an estimate of the highest power you can sustain for a prolonged hard effort. FTP-based zones translate percentages into personal wattage targets and primarily guide muscular endurance, pacing and power development.

  • Plan precise indoor and outdoor power targets
  • Compare absolute watts with watts per kilogram
  • Track changes using repeatable testing protocols
  • Use zones from recovery through neuromuscular power

LTHR

Lactate Threshold Heart Rate

LTHR estimates the heart rate around your sustainable threshold. LTHR-based zones help interpret cardiovascular strain and are valuable outdoors or whenever a heart-rate monitor is available, even without a power meter.

  • Monitor the internal cost of an effort
  • Guide endurance, tempo and threshold work
  • Compare heart-rate response with power output
  • Use a tested threshold—not the crude 220-minus-age formula

Tools with a reason

Useful calculations that lead to better decisions.

The calculators are not included just to produce more numbers. Each one answers a practical question before, during or after a ride.

FTP estimator

Set a realistic benchmark

Estimate FTP from several recognised effort protocols, then use the target in a real test. It gives newer riders a useful starting point and experienced riders a value they can validate and refine over time.

Zone calculation

Translate percentages instantly

Convert a percentage of FTP into watts, W/kg and its corresponding training zone. It makes workout planning quicker and shows exactly what an intensity adjustment means for your own profile.

Workout bias

Adjust the day, not the goal

Preview how changing an in-game workout bias alters target power. Lower the load when fatigue is high or increase it when appropriate, while seeing the revised watts, W/kg and zone before training.

Effort calories

Plan energy and fuelling

Estimate energy expenditure from average power and duration using mechanical work. Explore different ride scenarios to support fuelling and recovery planning, while recognising that the result is an estimate rather than a physiological measurement.

Zone reference

Keep every target visible

View FTP and LTHR ranges together, including percentages, watts, W/kg, heart rate and effort guidance. Export the personalised grid as a convenient reference for indoor sessions or offline use.

Live Ride

Apply the numbers while riding

Connect a compatible smart trainer and heart-rate monitor, follow live zone context and use Target mode for controlled power. Quick adjustments keep the session focused without hiding the rider behind data.

What this changes in practice

Learn. Adapt. Perform.

  1. 01

    Target the right adaptation

    Choose intensity for endurance, threshold, VO2 max, anaerobic capacity or sprint power rather than treating every workout alike.

  2. 02

    Use effort efficiently

    Stay close to the intended zone, reducing time spent too easy to stimulate adaptation or unnecessarily hard to recover well.

  3. 03

    Adapt to the rider

    Update FTP, LTHR and weight as fitness changes so targets continue to reflect the rider you are now.

  4. 04

    Measure progress

    Use consistent personal benchmarks to recognise improvements in power, cardiovascular response and power-to-weight ratio.
