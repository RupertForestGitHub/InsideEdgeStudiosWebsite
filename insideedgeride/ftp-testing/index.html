Different routes to an FTP estimate.

No field test is perfect. Repeat the same protocol under similar conditions and validate the result in normal threshold training.

ERG · PROGRESSIVE

Ramp Test

After the warm-up, ERG begins at 100 W and increases by 20 W every minute until the rider can no longer maintain the target.

75% × highest rolling 60-second power
  • Minute 12 · 320 W → FTP 240 W
  • Minute 13 · 340 W → FTP 255 W
  • Minute 14 · 360 W → FTP 270 W
Every second survived in the final step can improve the rolling-minute result.
PASSIVE · SUSTAINED

20-Minute Test

Ride one designated 20-minute interval at the strongest evenly paced power that can be sustained.

95% × 20-minute average power
  • 240 W average → FTP 228 W
  • 260 W average → FTP 247 W
  • 280 W average → FTP 266 W
Warm-up, preparation, recovery and cool-down data are excluded.
PASSIVE · TWO EFFORTS

2 × 8-Minute Test

Complete two maximal eight-minute efforts with a substantial recovery, then combine their recorded averages.

90% × mean of both averages
  • 250 + 270 W → FTP 234 W
  • 260 + 280 W → FTP 243 W
  • 280 + 300 W → FTP 261 W
Both test intervals need sufficient recorded power data for a result.

Built into the ride system

  2. 02

    Connect and ride

    Ramp uses fixed-watt ERG steps. Sustained tests lock to Passive mode so the rider—not the trainer—sets the maximal power.

  3. 03

    Calculate from the correct data

    Only the rolling ramp minute or designated sustained intervals feed the FTP estimate.

  4. 04

    Accept or review

    Save and share the report, or accept the estimated wattage as the new FTP across the app.

Why results differ

Every protocol measures through a different lens.

Anaerobically strong riders can score relatively highly in a ramp test. Sustained tests depend more on pacing and fatigue resistance. Treat each as an estimate, not a laboratory diagnosis.
